Backtester — BESS

Grid-scale battery (default 40 MWh / 20 MW) trading the Latvian day-ahead, mFRR, aFRR and intraday markets. Stateful 15-minute ISP simulation: state-of-charge is carried across periods, so what you discharge now limits what you can discharge later. Pick the battery's physical limits and operating band, toggle the strategies, and the optimiser sweeps every enabled strategy jointly. No look-ahead: day-ahead commits a day early, intraday closes 30 min and balancing bids 25 min before delivery — so every decision uses only prices settled by those gates (the newest settled balancing ISP is i−3); a period's own mFRR/aFRR prices decide only whether a placed, priced bid clears. Setup

Define the battery and the experiment environment — physical limits, the safe operating band (red zones), the round-trip margin we demand, and how the markets are bounded. Setup is not varied by the optimiser. "Reset to defaults" resets everything except the sim date range and the day-type filter.

mFRR ↔ aFRR adaptive offer split optimised

Per-direction routing of balancing volume between mFRR and aFRR that adapts over time (carried over from the wind-park backtester). Each direction starts at split x, and every w ISPs (the cadence, default 1 = every ISP) it re-checks which market paid more per MW over the trailing y-ISP lookback window — ending at the last settled ISP — and shifts toward the winner by step z. Up (discharge) and down (charge) are independent. Set z = 0 for a fixed split at x.

Day-ahead arbitrage (sell + buy-low) optimised

Day-ahead is committed a day in advance, so we don't know the clearing price yet — we use a simple lag-24h (96-ISP) persistence forecast. We place discharge offers at the day's forecast peaks (cleared when the actual price ≥ the sell floor) and buy-low offers at the day's forecast troughs (cleared when the actual price ≤ the buy ceiling) to pre-stock energy for the next peaks. The day's volume can be concentrated (few periods at full power) or spread (more periods at lower power). All quantities are whole MW.

Dynamic charging (real-time) optimised

Real-time refill from the markets that can be decided near delivery — mFRR-dn (25 min), aFRR-dn, intraday (30 min). Day-ahead troughs are "boring" (we still pay) and are handled by the committed buy-low leg above; here, balancing-down can go deeply negative, meaning the system pays us to absorb. All charge orders are priced at the max charge price and clear only if the realised price comes in at or below it. Routing uses known information only: the adaptive DOWN split shares the headroom between mFRR-dn and aFRR-dn; a share whose side was unusable in the last settled ISP re-routes to the other side; and intraday (whose quote is a pre-gate snapshot) takes the headroom only when settled balancing couldn't charge us at the ceiling at all. Off ⇒ no real-time charging.

Dynamic discharge pricing optimised

Don't sell at whatever the market pays. Every discretionary discharge offer carries an ask (break-even + min delta) and a leg clears only when its realised price reaches it. When the last settled mFRR price (ISP i−3 — the newest final price at the 25-min gate) has jumped by ≥ the threshold vs the lookback before it, we react by re-pricing the whole offer above that settled level (settled + mark-up): if the spike keeps running the full volume clears at the higher price; if it stalls the offer simply rests unfilled. No volume is ever withheld, and no unsettled price is ever consulted — re-pricing happens before the gate closes.

Opportunistic balancing override optimised

Alternative-cost logic — as a committed bet, not clairvoyance. If a period is already sold to DA and the last settled balancing price is running ≥ threshold above the live intraday quote, we buy the DA volume back on intraday (cost committed at the 30-min gate) and re-offer the freed energy to mFRR at quote + threshold before the 25-min gate. If the spike persists, the offer clears — DA revenue is kept and the gain is ≈ mFRR − intraday. If it fizzles, the offer rests, the energy stays in the battery and the period nets DA − intraday: a real, visible loss ("divert misses" in the counts). Imbalance is never a chosen close.

Operations & state-of-charge statistics

Cycling & duration

Average charge / discharge block length and the idle gap between them — how the battery paces itself given the "min time between direction change" and round-trip margin.

SoC safety audit

Time spent in the red zones — ISPs whose end-of-ISP state of charge is pinned at or inside the lower / upper red zone (within one whole-MW step of the boundary, so not even a 1-MW discretionary bid in that direction was possible; committed day-ahead legs may dig deeper inside). Dynamic with respect to the configured zones, not a fixed 0 % / 100 % check. And — most importantly — any committed balancing activation that could not be fulfilled. By design that must be zero: failing a balancing activation carries severe penalties, so the engine never offers more than the battery can deliver. A non-zero count here is a red flag.

Parameter sensitivity — what actually drives the optimum

Populated by ⚡ Optimise (adds ~20% to its runtime; per session, not persisted). Weight = the worst revenue loss from moving one parameter across its whole sweep range while the rest stay at the optimum — a parameter can be optimised to a dramatic-looking value (say, a rebalance wait of 660) yet carry almost no weight. The 1%-band is the contiguous range of values staying within 1% of the optimum: a wide band means the exact value is noise, not signal. Shape gives the safe direction (↑ / ↓ / interior peak); ⚠ marks an optimum sitting on the sweep boundary — usually the no-price-impact liquidity levers (treat as constraints, not free money); ✶ marks a sharp peak (one grid step already loses >5%). The global columns read the 3,000 seeded random samples instead: Spearman ρ (direction over the whole space) and where the top-10% of samples cluster — a cluster spanning the whole range means the parameter does not decide success. The interaction ranking scores parameter pairs by how non-additive their joint effect is over that population (parameters that only work in tandem), and the strongest pair is swept as a 2-D map around the optimum.
