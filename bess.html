Backtester — BESS

Grid-scale battery (default 40 MWh / 20 MW) trading the Latvian day-ahead, mFRR, aFRR and intraday markets. Stateful 15-minute ISP simulation: state-of-charge is carried across periods, so what you discharge now limits what you can discharge later. Pick the battery's physical limits and operating band, toggle the strategies, and the optimiser sweeps every enabled strategy jointly. Data covers ….

Setup

Define the battery and the experiment environment — physical limits, the safe operating band (red zones), the round-trip margin we demand, and how the markets are bounded. Setup is not varied by the optimiser and not touched by "Reset to defaults".

mFRR ↔ aFRR adaptive offer split optimised

Per-direction routing of balancing volume between mFRR and aFRR that adapts over time (carried over from the wind-park backtester). Each direction starts at split x, and every y ISPs it re-checks which market paid more per MW over the last y ISPs and shifts toward the winner by step z. Up (discharge) and down (charge) are independent. Set z = 0 for a fixed split at x.

Day-ahead arbitrage (sell + buy-low) optimised

Day-ahead is committed a day in advance, so we don't know the clearing price yet — we use a simple lag-24h (96-ISP) persistence forecast. We place discharge offers at the day's forecast peaks (cleared when the actual price ≥ the sell floor) and buy-low offers at the day's forecast troughs (cleared when the actual price ≤ the buy ceiling) to pre-stock energy for the next peaks. The day's volume can be concentrated (few periods at full power) or spread (more periods at lower power). All quantities are whole MW.

Dynamic charging (real-time) optimised

Real-time refill from the markets that can be decided near delivery — mFRR-dn (25 min), aFRR-dn, intraday (30 min). Day-ahead troughs are "boring" (we still pay) and are handled by the committed buy-low leg above; here, balancing-down can go deeply negative, meaning the system pays us to absorb. The mFRR-dn↔aFRR-dn routing follows the adaptive DOWN split, which shifts toward whichever pays more to absorb; a side is used only if its price is at or below the max charge price, with intraday as the fallback. Off ⇒ no real-time charging.

Dynamic discharge pricing optimised

Don't dump the whole battery the moment a price clears. If the recent mFRR/aFRR price trend is rising, hold back a fraction of the discharge now to capture an expected higher price a few ISPs later — there is still time because the balancing gate closes only 25 min before delivery, so bids more than 25 min out can be re-priced, re-sized or switched from mFRR to aFRR.

Opportunistic balancing override optimised

Alternative-cost logic. If we already sold a period to DA at, say, 150 €/MWh but a sudden balancing deficit pushes the price far above that, we divert the energy to the richer balancing product and close the day-ahead leg on intraday (or, if cheaper, take the imbalance settlement). We never divert so much that a committed balancing activation could go unfulfilled — only day-ahead positions are ever left to settle at imbalance.

Operations & state-of-charge statistics

Cycling & duration

Average charge / discharge block length and the idle gap between them — how the battery paces itself given the "min time between direction change" and round-trip margin.

SoC safety audit

Periods spent at the 0 % floor or 100 % ceiling, and — most importantly — any committed balancing activation that could not be fulfilled, with the cause. By design this must be zero: failing a balancing activation carries severe penalties, so the engine never offers more than the battery can deliver. A non-zero count here is a red flag.
