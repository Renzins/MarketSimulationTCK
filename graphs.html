Setup

Restricts the comparison to ISPs in this window. The aFRR window naturally starts ~2025-05-01 (before that, aFRR spreads are NaN and excluded). Day-type filter is independent from the mFRR / aFRR tabs.

Summary statistics

Headline numbers: how often each market fires, how often they fire in agreement, and the Pearson correlation between the two spreads. In the 4-s slot view (the default once the price data loads) the correlation runs over every POS+NEG slot entry; in the ISP-level fallback it uses ISPs where mFRR cleared AND aFRR-up had favourable activity. Each tile's own help line states its exact denominator.

Joint direction matrix

Rows: mFRR direction inferred from p_mfrr sign (Up ≥ +1, Dead band |·| < 1, Down ≤ −1). Columns: aFRR direction inferred from avg_p_pos / avg_p_neg being non-zero (Up only / Down only / Both directions / Neither). Diagonal-like cells (Up–Up, Down–Down) mean the markets agree.

Sign agreement of the DA-spread

For each 4-s aFRR slot where the directional clearing is defined, we record the sign of mFRR spread (= p_mfrr − p_da, broadcast from the encompassing ISP) and the sign of aFRR spread (= AST_POS − p_da on the left chart, AST_NEG − p_da on the right). Six mutually-exclusive segments: both positive (both markets above DA → upward signal), both negative (below DA → downward signal), the two mixed-sign disagreements, "both near DA" (both prices within ±10% of DA — signs not meaningful), and "aFRR N/A" (that direction never activated in the slot). Note on data: these slot-level charts use the raw per-4-s AST_POS / AST_NEG clearings — the favourable-only filter (which drops unfavourable slots) is applied only in the L3 P&L backtest, not here. So "aFRR-down" can land in any of the four buckets, including both positive (this happens when AST_NEG itself cleared above DA — the system priced curtailment at a level the wind park wouldn't bid into for profit, but the market still cleared there). During the brief window where the page falls back to per-ISP averages (while the chunked 4-s file is still loading), the favourable-filtered values DO force the right chart into "both negative" — that fallback distribution is not market truth.

mFRR / DA spread when aFRR is N/A

For 4-s slots where the corresponding aFRR direction was not activated (the AST_{POS,NEG} field was null upstream), the sign-agreement chart above can't compare two spreads — there's only the mFRR price. These charts show how mFRR positioned itself vs DA in those silent-aFRR slots: above DA (green), near DA within ±10% (yellow), or below DA (red). Together with the gray N/A slice in the chart above, they answer: "when aFRR didn't fire, was mFRR signalling anything?"

Setup

Reserve prices are hourly EUR/MW·h capacity prices for Latvia. Each market's daily value is the mean (over the window's ISPs of the chosen day type) of its up/down-average price, after winsorisation — so mFRR and aFRR each have one value per day.

Summary statistics

Headline numbers across the daily series: how the two reserve markets co-move (price levels and day-over-day changes), how persistent the aFRR−mFRR spread is, and how each market's price correlates with the day's Baltic imbalance magnitude.

Daily reserve price — mFRR vs aFRR

One point per day: the average reserve capacity price for each market. Watch whether the two lines track each other or drift into separate regimes (one market systematically cheaper for a stretch).

aFRR vs mFRR daily price — co-movement

Each point is a day, plotted at (mFRR price, aFRR price) and coloured by date. A tight diagonal cloud ⇒ the two move in unison; a shapeless or anti-diagonal cloud ⇒ they're independent or substitutes. The Pearson r is in the title.

Reserve-price spread (aFRR − mFRR) over time

Positive ⇒ aFRR capacity is the pricier product that day; negative ⇒ mFRR is pricier. Long runs on one side of zero indicate persistent regimes (a slow tug-of-war); rapid sign-flips would indicate a fast day-to-day cat-and-mouse. Lag-1 autocorrelation of this series is reported in the summary above.

Capacity premium vs realized activation value

The capacity price is an option premium; the activation energy is its payoff. Each point is a day: x = that day's DOWN capacity premium (daily mean price × 24 h, EUR/MW·day), y = a realized activation-payoff proxy for 1 MW offered all day — mFRR-dn: 0.25 h × Σ max(0, −pmFRR) over the day's ISPs (energy earned absorbing at negative prices); aFRR-dn: 0.25 × Σ max(0, −avg pneg) — no extra dispatch factor, because avg pneg is the favourable-slot sum ÷ 225, so the partial-dispatch dilution is already inside the price (the same convention the backtester's aFRR revenue uses). The proxy ignores award probability and positive-price buy-backs — read it as relative option value, not P&L. Points far below the diagonal = premium paid richly vs what activations returned; far above = capacity was cheap insurance. The premium axis respects the capacity winsor pairs above; the payoff proxy uses the activation energy price winsor pair (raw mFRR has ±10 000 €/MWh outliers — one ISP can add 2 500 €/MW·day to a day's proxy). These scatters have no time axis; the structural-break markers (dotted red = Baltic desynchronization, 8 Feb 2025; shaded = aFRR market ramp-up, Feb–Apr 2025) live on this tab's date-axis charts.

Baltic imbalance magnitude by reserve-price quartile

Days are split into quartiles by each market's daily reserve price (Q1 = cheapest, Q4 = priciest); the box plots show the distribution of that day's mean |Baltic imbalance| (MW). If pricier reserve days sit higher, a high reserve price flags elevated extreme-surplus / deficit risk. The two panels let you see whether the signal lives in the mFRR price, the aFRR price, or both.

Setup

Balancing prices are driven by surprises and timing, not by forecast levels. This tab conditions the mFRR spread on forecast errors (actual − forecast) and forecast revisions (knowable before the gate), exposes the within-hour and hour-of-day structure, measures which signals lead which, and turns it all into conditional spike probabilities. Charts that need fundamentals require data-fund.js (preprocess-fundamentals.py); the timing charts work from the core dataset alone.

Spread by forecast ERROR — the surprise, not the level

mFRR spread (PmFRR − PDA, winsorized) box-plotted by quantile bins of three surprise variables: Baltic wind error (actual − DA forecast; negative = wind under-delivered), Baltic load error (actual − DA forecast; positive = more demand than planned), and the pre-gate knowable LV+LT wind forecast revision (intraday − day-ahead; Estonia publishes no intraday wind forecast, so the revision is defined on the LV+LT footprint). If the error charts slope and the level charts on the mFRR tab don't, the market prices surprises — and the revision chart tells you how much of that is knowable in time to bid on it.

Hour-of-day & slot-in-hour structure

Left: median mFRR spread by UTC hour with the interquartile band — the daily rhythm (morning/evening ramps). Right: the within-hour sawtooth — day-ahead trades hourly blocks while physics ramps continuously, so the :00/:15/:30/:45 ISPs of the hour carry systematically different imbalance and spread. Bars = median spread per slot; line = mean |Baltic imbalance| per slot. A visible slot pattern is deterministic, knowable structure.

Monthly spike monitor — is the market stationary?

Per calendar month: the share of ISPs where the RAW mFRR price (no winsorisation) exceeds the up-spike threshold, the share below the down-spike threshold, and the mean size of the up-exceedance (line, right axis). The dataset spans the Baltic desynchronization (Feb 2025, the first bar) — if spike frequency drifts month to month, pooled statistics and backtest optima are blending different regimes.

Lead–lag — which signals move first?

Cross-correlation of three candidate leaders against the (winsorized) mFRR spread at ISP lags −8…+8. A peak at a positive lag means that series LEADS the mFRR spread by that many ISPs. The backtesters may only bid on prices settled ≥ 3 ISPs before delivery — bars at lag ≥ 3 are exploitable signal; a peak at lag 0 that dies by lag 3 is contemporaneous correlation you can see but not trade.

Conditional spike probability

P(RAW mFRR price beyond the threshold) conditioned on the Baltic imbalance regime (±30 MW dead band), the Baltic wind-error tercile, and the UTC hour block — the bidding aid: "given what kind of period this is, how often does the market spike?" A positive threshold counts up-spikes (≥ X); a negative threshold counts down-spikes (≤ X). Cells show probability and sample count; deeper red = more frequent. NB: the imbalance regime and the error are contemporaneous — this table explains; the revision chart above is the knowable counterpart.

Merit-order scarcity & cross-zone divergence

Left: the LV accepted SA-mFRR bid band (daily mean of max-bid − min-bid, upward direction) and where the clearing price sits inside it (0 = at the floor, 1 = at the ceiling; daily mean over up-clearing ISPs). A narrowing band with the clearing price riding its ceiling = a thinning stack — scarcity pricing before it shows in averages. Right: the share of ISPs per day where the LV mFRR price decouples from Estonia / Lithuania by more than the threshold — cross-zone divergence marks congestion episodes where LV-specific scarcity (and LV-specific opportunity) appears.
